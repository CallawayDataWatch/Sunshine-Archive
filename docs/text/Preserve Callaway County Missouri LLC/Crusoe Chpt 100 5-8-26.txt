Re:  Proposed Chapter 100  Industrial Development Revenue Bond Agreement —  Crusoe 
Technologies Data  Center Project, Callaway County, Missouri 

Dear Callaway  County  Commissioners: 

Crusoe  Technologies  LLC  (“Crusoe”)  is  pleased to  present this formal  request for  a  Chapter  100 
Industrial  Development  Revenue  Bond  (“Chapter  100”)  agreement  with  Callaway  County, 
Missouri.  Crusoe  is  a  technology  infrastructure  company  focused  on  purpose-built,  sustainable 
data  centers  and  high-performance  computing  facilities.  We  are  actively  evaluating  Callaway 
County  as  the  site  for  a  significant  capital  investment  that  would  bring  lasting  economic  and 

fiscal  benefits  to  the  region. 

We  respectfully  propose  the  following  terms  and  set forth  below  our  substantive justifications  for 

those  terms,  along  with  a  summary  of the  projected  fiscal  impact  (“85%  PDF’). 

|.  PROPOSED CHAPTER  100  TERMS 

Agreement Term: 25  years from  the  date  of project  completion. 

Abatement  Percentage: 85%  abatement  on  real  property  and  personal  property taxes 
assessed  against the  project during  the  agreement term. 

Property  Subject to  Abatement: All  real  property  comprising  the  data  center facility  and 
associated  on-site  improvements,  and  all  personal  property,  including  servers,  networking 
equipment,  and  other  qualifying  tangible  personal  property  placed  in  service  at the  site. 
PILOT &  Community  Benefit Payments:  Payments  in  Lieu  of Taxes (“PILOT”) and  Community 
Benefit  payments will  be  structured  in  collaboration  with  the  taxing jurisdictions  to  direct 
meaningful,  recurring  revenue to  the  community. 

Jobs Commitment:  Crusoe  shall  commit to  creating  and  maintaining  an  agreed  upon  minimum 
number  of full-time  equivalent jobs  at  the  facility,  with  an  average  annual  wage  equal  to  or 
exceeding  150%  of the  average  annual  wage  in  Callaway  County,  as  determined  by  reference 
to  applicable  Missouri  Department  of Economic  Development data. 

Fees:  Crusoe  shall  be  responsible for  reasonable  and  documented  legal  fees  and  expenses of 
the  County  related  to  the  Project,  including  Gilmore  &  Bell,  P.C., bond  counsel  to  the  County, 
and  the  selected  bond  trustee.

lll.  JUSTIFICATION FOR  PROPOSED TERMS 

Crusoe  respectfully  submits  that  the  proposed  25-year  term  and  85%  abatement  are 
well-supported  by  the  specific  economic  and  development characteristics  of this  project  and  the 

Callaway  County  market.  The following  factors,  taken  together, distinguish  this  project from  sites 

in  competing jurisdictions: 

1.  Higher  Property Tax Rate  Than  Comparable Sites 

Callaway  County’s  effective  property  tax  rate  on  commercial  and  industrial  real  and  personal 

property 

is  materially  higher  than 

that  of  comparable  data  center  sites  under  active 

consideration  in  Missouri  and  the  nation.  The  85%  abatement  corrects  for  this  structural  cost 

disparity  and  places the  County  on  an  equal  footing  with  competing  sites. 

2.  Hyperscaler Tenant Requirements  &  Site  Competitiveness 

Crusoe  is  pursuing  a  hyperscaler  tenant  for  this  facility.  The  tenant  will  be  a  large-scale, 

credit-worthy  technology  company  with  the  financial  capacity  to  make  substantial  ongoing 

investments  in  GPU  and  computing  infrastructure  at  the  site.  Hyperscaler tenants  operate  under 

rigorous 

internal 

return-on-capital 

frameworks  and  evaluate  competing 

sites  on 

a 

total-cost-of-ownership  basis.  An  abatement  structure  that  keeps  the  all-in  occupancy  cost 

competitive  is  often  a  threshold  requirement  for  site  selection.  Securing  such  a  tenant  is  critical 

not  only  to  the  viability  of  this  project,  but  to  maximizing  the  long-term  capital  investment  and 

associated  economic  activity  in  Callaway  County. 

3.  Higher  Utility  &  Transmission Costs 

Data  center  operations  are  energy-intensive.  The  utility  and  transmission  cost  structure  for  this 

prospective  project  is  higher  than  in  certain  competing  markets,  adding  meaningful  operating 

cost  pressure to  the  project on  an  annual  basis. 

4.  Higher  Public  Improvement Costs 

Development  of  this  site  will  require  significant  public  infrastructure  improvements.  These 

include  road  improvements  to  accommodate  the  construction  trafÏc  and  ongoing  operational 

logistics  associated  with  a  data  center  campus,  as  well  as  a  necessary  waterline  upgrade  to 

ensure  adequate  water  supply  and  pressure  for  cooling  and  fire  suppression  systems.  These 
capital  costs,  borne  directly  by  Crusoe,  represent a  real  and  material  cost to  develop the  project.

5.  Structuring  Payments  to  Keep Money  Local 

The  Chapter  100  structure  affords  considerable  flexibility  in  how  PILOT and  Community  Benefit 
payments  are  allocated  and  directed.  Crusoe  is  committed  to  working  collaboratively  with 

Callaway  County  and  the  other  taxing  jurisdictions  to  design  a  payment  structure  that  is 

consistent with  applicable  law  and  delivers  meaningful,  recurring  revenue to  local  entities. 

6.  Comparable  Data  Center Chapter  100 Agreement terms  in  Missouri 

Crusoe  believes  the  proposed  abatement  terms  are  more  competitive  and  result  in  higher 

community  benefit than  other  recently  approved  Chapter  100  Bond  Agreements.  For  example: 

e  Nebius  — Independence,  Missouri  (Jackson  County) Approved  March  2026 

o  20-year  term  at  98%  abatement  for  both  real  and  90%  abatement  for  personal 

property  taxes 

e  Mica —  Kansas  City,  Missouri  (Port  KC) Approved  in  July 2025 

o  25-year  term  with  a  75%  abatement  on  real  and  personal  property  taxes, 

complemented  by  a  100%  sales  tax  exemption  for  all  construction  materials  and 

project  equipment. 

IV.  NEXT  STEPS 

Crusoe  welcomes  the  opportunity  to  present  these  terms  in  greater  detail  to  the  Commission 

and  its  advisors  and  to  work  cooperatively  toward  a  mutually  beneficial  agreement.  We  are 

prepared  to  engage  directly  with  any  relevant  stakeholders  to  advance  this  process  and  work 

collaboratively with  the  community. 

We  look  forward  to  a  continued  and  productive  dialogue  regarding  this  important  investment 
opportunity  for  Callaway  County.  Please  do  not  hesitate  to  contact  us  at  your  earliest 

convenience.
